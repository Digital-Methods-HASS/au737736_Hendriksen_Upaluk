Some wrangling:

  • Split up pages into separate lines (separated by \n) using stringr::str_split()
  • Unnest into regular columns using tidyr::unnest()
  • Remove leading/trailing white space with stringr::str_trim()
got_df <- data.frame(got_text) %>% 
  mutate(text_full = str_split(got_text, pattern = '\n')) %>% 
  unnest(text_full) %>%

What are the most common meaningful words and what emotions do you expect will dominate this volume? Are there any terms that are similarly ambiguous to the ‘confidence’ above?

The most common meaningful words in the chart highlight a world driven by power, family, and mortality. Terms like lord, king, brother, mother, and child emphasize the importance of lineage and relationships, while death, die, dark, and fire reinforce the constant presence of danger. Words such as time and watch suggest inevitability and tension building toward future conflict.

From this, the dominant emotions are clearly fear, anticipation, sadness, and anger. Fear is reflected in repeated mentions of death, fire, and watch, suggesting vigilance and threat. Anticipation, led by time, conveys a sense that major events are always approaching. Sadness appears through leave, fell, and dark, indicating loss and emotional weight. Anger emerges in harsher language like bastard and conflict-related terms. However, emotions like trust and joy persist through words such as mother, child, and smile, hinting at human connection amid chaos.

Several words are emotionally ambiguous, similar to “confidence.” Watch can imply protection or danger, fire can symbolize destruction or strength, and sweet may be sincere or ironic. Even lord spans multiple emotional categories, making it context-dependent.

Overall, the sentiment suggests a harsh, unstable world balanced by moments of loyalty and connection.
